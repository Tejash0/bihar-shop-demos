Why this can work here

The gap

Quick-commerce apps grow city by city, starting with the biggest. Until they reach our area, people keep buying cleaning items at the kirana shop, and modern wellness brands only reach them through Amazon, days later. That leaves a few years to build a local name.

The catch

Those apps stay away partly because people here spend less per order. So we can’t copy their model as it is. We have to sell smaller packs, keep delivery cheap and let people pay at the door.

How the demos handle it

  • ₹10-29 sachets and trial packs, so trying costs almost nothing
  • Free delivery from ₹149-299, not ₹500+
  • Cash or UPI at the door. No card needed, no online payment
  • Order on WhatsApp for people who don’t like forms
  • Combos and refills that show the money saved

Our pick: our own site at ₹0 for local delivery orders, with Amazon for buyers anywhere else in India, which is the owner’s plan. Each product on the site links to its Amazon listing. Move to Shopify only if monthly online sales grow enough to justify its fees.

Suggested plan

  1. Start with cleaning

    Every home buys it every month, it needs no special licence beyond GST and correct labels, and it builds a list of customers and a delivery route. Margins are thin, so keep the area tight.

  2. Test on WhatsApp for 4-6 weeks

    About 30 products, one area, cash on delivery, orders in a Google Sheet. Share the demo link as a catalogue. Spend on stock, not technology.

  3. Put the website live

    Once there are steady repeat orders, launch the real site on a .in domain (about ₹550). The demo code is already most of it.

  4. Add supplements on the same delivery route

    Higher margin, but it needs an FSSAI licence, a contract manufacturer and more trust-building. The riders and customers from step 1 make it cheaper to launch.

  5. Automate at about 30 orders a day

    Add the order database, WhatsApp updates and online payment (Razorpay, using the existing company for its checks). That is the ₹4,000-6,000 a month stage.

Is there a better option?

Supply the kirana shops instead of homes. Selling our own-label cleaning packs wholesale to 50 local shops avoids most delivery cost and uses shops people already trust. It can run alongside the home delivery shop, and Chamak’s bulk-order form is built for it.

Making it ourselves, selling in our shop and on Amazon

Making the products ourselves is where the profit is, and concentrates are cheap to ship. These are the problems to solve before the first batch, most serious first.

Home production rules were the least clear part of the research. Confirm the handwash licence, the Legal Metrology registration and any pollution-board permission with a CA before buying stock.

Licences and rules to know

Supplements

  • Sold as a health supplement, ashwagandha and similar herbs fall under FSSAI. That means a food business licence and the health-supplement rules.
  • No disease claims (“cures anxiety”, “boosts immunity against…”). Claims like that turn it into an Ayurvedic medicine, which needs a state AYUSH drug licence.
  • The easiest start is a contract manufacturer that already holds these licences, selling under our brand.

Cleaning products

  • GST registration. The existing company may already cover this.
  • Packs need the legal label details: MRP, net quantity, maker’s name and address, and customer-care contact.
  • For own-label packs, get the supplier’s quality and safety documents in writing.

This is a starting checklist, not legal advice. Confirm with a CA or licensing consultant before launch.
